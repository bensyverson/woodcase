# The short list, to read

Each concept with its source directions verbatim (the agents' own words; palette and type are theirs, and a build would choose again). Ordered by my recommendation, not by score; scores are in `shortlist.md`. For K06 and K02, which several agents reached, two to four members are shown.

## K12 · Sight earned

**agent-2: Resolution**

The deck gains sight as it goes. The first slides are drawn only as what an agent can read, text and numbers, and each slide renders a little more until the last are full-color, finished pixels. The idea is the product's promise played out over time: from blind, to text-described (`tree`), to seen (`shot`). The ground starts pure black and brightens slide by slide through grays to a clean white by the outlets slide. Color is absent until the loop's `shot`, where the banking app appears as the deck's first color; after that, color stays. Type moves with it: slides 1–2 are all Martian Mono at 36px, gray on black. From the loop on, Instrument Sans enters for headlines at 84px/600, and the mono recedes to a supporting role. The gap is a black page with a line of gray text, `frame  banking-home  0,0 402×874`, and nothing else: a design that exists only as coordinates. The loop is the turn: the left half is the same screen as `tree` text, the right half is the same screen as `shot` pixels, split by a hard vertical edge. JavaScript sits on mid-gray, a script at left and a shaded region at right showing the before/after layout it measured. The outlets are on white: one small card rendered five ways in a row. The reveal breaks the arc by going back to black: the whole deck's activity log as text, the deck returning to how its author saw it. Before reading, the reader sees darkness with one line of faint type, and on flipping, a page that gets lighter.

**agent-4: Agent's Eye**

The idea is that the deck is shown the way an agent perceives design: as text first. Nearly every slide contains no drawing at all, only text laid out as `tree` lays it out, and pictures appear only where `shot` would be called, as the last mile. The discipline runs through every slide: an image must be earned the way a screenshot is. The ground is a pale gray-green (#E9ECE6), unlit and flat, the ink a dark olive-black (#1D211C), with one muted signal orange (#D9642B) reserved for ids and revs. Type is JetBrains Mono for everything, at a single size of 30 pt on a strict character grid, with hierarchy made only by weight and by capitals; there is no second typeface. The gap is a slide holding only a human prompt and an agent's reply that admits it cannot see, set as two blocks of text. The loop is the real banking `tree` output set large, filling the page, with three annotations in orange; the `shot` beat is the first image in the deck, a single rendered header sitting in the grid exactly where its row would be. JavaScript is a script and a two-line transcript, no chrome. The outlets are a list of five lines, each a verb and a destination. The reveal is the only slide with color beyond orange: this deck's own tree, with its slides as rows, each row paired with a thumbnail of the slide it describes. Before reading, the reader sees an almost blank gray page of evenly set monospace, and senses that text is the medium.

## K20 · Onion skin

**agent-5: Onion skin**

Every slide shows its own history: the current state in full ink and its earlier revisions ghosted behind it at stepped opacities, 40%, 20%, 10%, offset by the few pixels each edit moved them, like an animator's onion skin, so iteration is not described but seen on every page. The ground is a cool near-white (#F6F7F9), the current revision is ink (#101216), the ghosts are the same ink at lower opacity with a one-step background blur on the oldest, and the only color is a lime marker (#B8E62E) under the revision token that labels the current layer. Type is Hanken Grotesk 600 at 100 pt for headlines, and Commit Mono 20 pt for tokens and literals; every headline's ghosts are its real earlier drafts, taken from the log. The gap shows a sentence with no ghosts at all, frozen, the one page where nothing iterates; the loop shows a banking header whose ghosts step toward the final layout in three moves, each labeled `set` with its token; JavaScript shows a heading whose ghost is wider than the trim and is struck through, the draft the script measured and threw away, with the current one fitting; the outlets show one card whose ghosts are the same card as React, SwiftUI and PDF renders, fanned. The reveal breaks the pattern by laying every ghost of the whole deck side by side in time order across one page, labeled by activity-log line, so the reader sees the deck being made. Before reading, the reader sees faint echoes behind firm shapes and senses motion in a still PDF.

## K17 · Loop spine

**agent-4: Loop Spine**

The idea is one diagram that never leaves: a ring of three nodes, Command, View, Iterate, sits in the same place on every slide, drawn as a 360 px circle with three dots on the left third of the frame, and each slide lights the part of the loop it is about while the right two thirds explain. The ring is the navigation, the thesis and the slide number at once. The ground is deep ink blue (#0E1A2B), the ring a 3 px stroke in 30% white, the active arc drawn with an angular gradient from teal (#3EE6C1) to white that sweeps only the segment in play, with a soft outer glow on the lit node. Type is Space Grotesk at 84 pt, weight 500, for the one headline, and Space Mono 26 pt for everything the tool prints. The gap is the ring broken: the arc between View and Iterate is missing, a physical gap in the circle, with "Agents can't see what they changed." The loop lights each node in turn across one slide, with the real `tree`, `set` and `shot` lines placed next to their nodes. JavaScript draws a second, small ring inside the first, spinning the whole loop in one transaction, with the script beside it. Built with its users makes the ring thick and shows attributed writes as small ticks around its circumference. The outlets break the ring's containment: five spokes run from its center out to the right edge, each ending in CGContext, PNG, PDF, React or SwiftUI. The reveal traces the ring once for every entry in the deck's activity log, so the circle becomes a dense spiral of hairlines, with the log beside it. Before reading, the reader sees the same glowing circle on every page, partly lit.

## K06 · Revisions

**agent-3: One Rectangle**

The idea is the smallest possible demonstration: the whole deck is one rectangle, and each slide is that rectangle after one more `set`, so the story of the loop is told by a single node changing a single property at a time, and the text on each slide is only the command that produced it. The ground is pure white; the rectangle starts as a 1 px gray outline and ends, by the last slide, as a finished card in one saturated blue `#2F5BFF`; there is no light but what the rectangle acquires (an outer shadow appears on one slide as its own `set`). Type is none as design: every slide carries one line of JetBrains Mono 400 at 30 px bottom-left, the literal command, and nothing else, so type is not the point. The gap is the empty outline with the command `woodcase shot ?`; the loop is three slides, `tree` printing the rectangle's single row, `set` giving it a fill, `shot` naming the PNG; JavaScript is the one slide where the rectangle changes several properties at once, justified by a measure in the script, and the command is a single `woodcase js` line; the outlets slide breaks the pattern first, the rectangle shown five times, as pixels, as a vector PDF page's outline, as a JSX line, as a SwiftUI line and as a CGContext call; the reveal breaks it for good, the rectangle stepping aside to show this deck's full activity log, whose every line is one of the previous slides. Before reading, the reader sees a white page with a single shape on it, and on the next page, the same shape changed.

**agent-2: Flipbook**

The whole deck is one frame being edited. Every slide is the *same* layout at a later revision, one `set` (or one `js`) away from the previous page. The command that made the change is printed at the top, and the changed node is outlined. The idea is the loop made physical: flipping the PDF is iterating, and the reader watches the deck converge. The ground is a steady warm white (#F5F3EF) throughout, because it never changes. Color arrives only when a `set` adds it: the first `fill` command introduces a single vermilion (#E4572E) that stays for the rest. Type is also subject to editing. The first slide is the default Inter at 32px; one slide's `set … fontFamily "Bricolage Grotesque"` changes the headline face mid-deck, in front of the reader. The running head is always `rev <token>  set <node> <prop> <value>` in Geist Mono at 18px. The gap is revision 1: an unstyled frame with the sentence in default type, visibly unfinished. The loop is the next three revisions, where each slide is literally one iteration and the `tree` row for the changed node appears as a strip at the bottom. The JavaScript slide is the one where many things change at once, with the running head showing a `js` command and a count of 23 writes in 1 transaction, outlines around every changed node. The outlets slide is the same frame beside its generated SwiftUI source. The pattern breaks at the reveal: instead of another revision, the page shows every previous revision as small thumbnails in a grid with their rev tokens: the flipbook seen all at once. Before reading, the reader sees a plain, almost default slide with one thing outlined, and senses something is about to change.

**agent-1: Revisions**

The deck is one artifact improved nine times: every slide is the same 1920×1080 composition — a title card for Woodcase — at a successive revision, beginning broken (overflowing text, misaligned, clipped, as `tree` would flag with `⚠ clipped`) and ending finished on the install slide, so the reader watches the loop run instead of hearing it described. Ground starts as raw white with default black Helvetica-like system type and harsh red lint warnings, and converges revision by revision toward the final palette: a deep aubergine ground (#2A1B2E) with lilac (#C9B6E4) and one saffron (#F5B83D) accent. Final typefaces are Syne at 160 px for the wordmark and Geist Mono for all annotations; the earlier revisions intentionally use the renderer's fallback so the typography itself arrives as an edit. Each slide shows the card large, with a margin strip on the right in Geist Mono listing the rev token, the one command that produced this revision, and what `tree` said before it. The gap is revision 0: an agent's unseen first draft, text overflowing its frame, and the caption "it compiled." The loop is revisions 1–3 on one slide as three stacked thumbnails, each with its `tree` warning and its `set`. JavaScript is revision 4: one script that measured the title, found it clipping, and threw — the card is unchanged and the rev token is the same. Outlets is revision 7: the finished card shown as PDF, PNG and as a SwiftUI preview frame. The reveal breaks the pattern: every revision side by side in a contact sheet with the real rev tokens from this deck's own history. Before reading, the reader sees something visibly wrong — a broken title card — and keeps going to see it fixed.

**agent-4: Fidelity Ramp**

The idea is that the deck iterates in front of the reader: slide one is a gray wireframe, the boxes and labels an agent reads in `tree`, and each slide gains one layer of fidelity (type, then color, then light, then effects) until the reveal is fully finished. The whole deck is the loop, command, view, iterate, played at the scale of the deck itself, and every slide's style is literally one step further than the last. The ground begins as white with 1 px gray rects labeled by name and `w×h`, and ends as a deep, fully rendered composition: a mesh gradient in indigo, coral and sand, soft inner shadows, a background blur behind glass cards. Type begins as a system mono placeholder, arrives as Instrument Sans at slide three, and gains Instrument Serif italic for one emphasized word per slide from slide five on. The gap is pure wireframe: two boxes, "code" solid and "design" dashed with no fill. The loop is three wireframe cards acquiring real type as they go left to right. JavaScript is the slide where color arrives, and the script is the thing that applied it. The outlets are the slide where effects arrive, five cards each with a different treatment, rendering the same card into five destinations. The reveal breaks the ramp by stepping back: all slides shown small, in order, as a strip, with the activity log beneath each one naming the commands between it and the next. Before reading, the reader sees an unfinished page, and in paging, sees it finish.

## K02 · Self-annotated (X-ray)

**agent-3: X-Ray**

The idea is that the deck shows its own layout: every visible element on every slide wears a hairline bounding box and a small tag with its id and rect, exactly as `tree` reports them, so the reader sees the machine's view of each page on top of the human one and the thesis is never stated, only seen. The ground is a cool off-white `#EEF0F2`; content is ink `#14161A`; the annotation layer is a single magenta `#FF2E88` at 1 px stroke with 13 px tags on magenta fills, and absolute-positioned so it never disturbs layout; no shadows, no gradients. Type is Newsreader 500 at 88 px for claims (a serif to keep the human layer distinctly human), IBM Plex Mono 500 at 13 px for the tags and 24 px for any command. The gap slide has a headline and a single framed placeholder, and the annotation on the placeholder reads `frame ? 0,0 ?×?`: the agent cannot see it; the loop puts `tree` output in a column whose rows are themselves annotated, the text and its tags agreeing; JavaScript shows one headline whose width tag is measured and a magenta ruler asserting it fits, next to the script that made the check; the outlets slide places one small card five times, each tagged with the outlet it went to; the reveal breaks the pattern by hiding the content layer entirely, leaving only the magenta boxes, which still spell out the page, followed by the activity log that placed them. Before reading, the reader sees pale pages covered in fine pink rectangles, like an inspector left switched on.

**agent-5: Debug overlay**

Every slide is drawn with its own layout debugger switched on: each element carries a hairline bounding box and a tiny label with its node name, rect and five-character id, exactly the columns `tree` prints, so the reader is shown on every page what an agent reads without being told. The ground is white; content is in black; the overlay is a thin magenta (#FF2D9B) at 1 px with labels in 12 pt, the color of layout debuggers everywhere, and padding and gap regions are hatched in 10%-opacity magenta; there is no light, no shadow. Type is Geist at 88 pt 600 for headlines and Geist Mono at 12 pt for the overlay labels, which are real values read back with `tree` after layout. The gap slide shows a single sentence whose bounding box is labeled `rect ?` and `id ?`, the only unknowns in the deck; the loop slide shows the five `tree` rows with each row's box connected by a magenta leader to its rendered rect in a banking-header rendering; JavaScript shows a headline whose overlay records two widths, the one before and after the script measured and shrank it; the outlets slide shows one card with its overlay and three code excerpts whose boxes are labeled with their file paths. The reveal breaks the pattern by turning the overlay off for the first time: a clean page with only the activity log and the line "every box you saw was read, not drawn by hand." Before reading, the reader sees pink hairlines around everything and senses the scaffolding.

## K18 · Exploded pipeline

**agent-5: Exploded pipeline**

The deck is one diagram, the pipeline, built up a stage per slide as an exploded stack of planes: parse, resolve, expand, layout, render, drawn as parallelogram polygons receding in isometric depth, so the reader always knows where in the engine they are. The ground is a cool pale gray (#E9ECEF) with a blueprint-quiet dot grid of 2 px dots at 40 px; planes are translucent white at 70% opacity with a 1 px slate stroke and a soft inner shadow, and each stage's plane lights in a signal teal (#0FA3B1) when it is the subject. Type is Manrope, 700 for 72 pt headlines, 400 for 24 pt labels set along the plane edges, with Fira Code for any literal. The gap shows the stack with only the bottom plane (render) filled and a dashed empty channel running up from it back to an agent glyph: no way back. The loop turns that channel into a closed arc of three arrows labeled `tree`, `set`, `shot`, touching the layout and render planes; JavaScript places a bracket around the whole stack labeled "one transaction", with a small red stop mark where a throw would abort; the outlets slide fans five thin arrows off the render plane to CGContext, PNG, PDF, React and SwiftUI labels. The reveal breaks the isometric view: it flattens to a straight-on page showing the stack as a single plane, the deck file itself, with its log beside it. Before reading, the reader sees a technical drawing of layers and expects rigor.
