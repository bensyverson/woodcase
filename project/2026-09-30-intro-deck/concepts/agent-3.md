# Intro deck: ten directions (agent 3)

## Glow Keynote

The idea is the launch-event register every developer tool borrows: one claim per slide, very large, over a single piece of evidence, so the whole deck reads as a keynote someone forgot to present. The ground is near-black `#0B0C10` with one soft radial gradient per slide (a desaturated violet-to-transparent bloom, about 1400 px wide, placed off-center behind the evidence), and every piece of evidence sits in a rounded 24 px card of `#15171E` with a 1 px `#FFFFFF14` stroke and a 60 px outer shadow, which gives the only "light" in the room. Type is Inter Tight 700 for headlines at 112 px, tracked −3%, set two lines maximum in white, with Inter 400 at 32 px in 60% white beneath; code is JetBrains Mono 500 at 26 px. The gap is a headline ("Agents write the code. They can't see the design.") over an empty card with a dashed stroke; the loop is three cards in a row, `tree`, `set`, `shot`, each with its real output, joined by thin arrow paths; JavaScript is one tall card of a `woodcase js` script with the `throw` line highlighted by a linear-gradient underlay; the outlets slide is a hub diagram, a central "Swift library" pill with five spokes to CGContext, PNG, PDF, React, SwiftUI; the reveal breaks the pattern by dropping the card and letting the activity log fill the frame edge to edge in mono. Before reading, the reader sees a dark field, a glow, and a bright rectangle, the house style of every AI launch of the last three years.

## Terminal Session

The idea is that the deck is a single terminal transcript cut into nine screens: every slide is a window of scrollback, prompts and output only, and the narrative lives in `#` comments typed between commands. The ground is a terminal black `#101010`, with text in a warm off-white `#E6E1D6`, prompts in a muted green `#7FB069`, comments in 45% gray, and one amber `#F2A541` reserved for revision tokens wherever they appear, so the reader learns to follow the rev across slides; there is no light beyond the text itself. Type is IBM Plex Mono throughout, one size (34 px, 1.45 line height, a 100-column grid that fills 1920 with 80 px margins), with emphasis only by weight (600) and color, never size, except the title slide, which runs the product name at 34 px like everything else and lets the empty screen do the work. The gap is a lone comment, `# the agent wrote 2,000 lines of SwiftUI. what does it look like?`, followed by a cursor block and nothing; the loop is literally `tree`, `set --rev …`, `shot` with their real outputs, the shot output line naming a PNG; JavaScript is a heredoc of `woodcase js` ending with the script throwing and the exit line showing nothing was written; the outlets slide is five short commands, `render --format pdf`, `generate react`, `generate swiftui`, each followed by the file listing it produced; the reveal breaks the pattern by being the one slide with a second color field, the rendered first slide of the deck appearing as a small inset "shot" in the lower right while the transcript above is `woodcase activity` on this very file. Before reading, the reader sees a black page with lines of text and a blinking-cursor rectangle: a terminal, which tells them who the deck is for.

## Swiss Poster

The idea is the International Typographic Style applied without apology: every slide is a poster built on one 12-column grid, one message per slide set enormous, with the product's text-first nature carried by typography rather than illustration. The ground is a flat paper white `#F4F2EE`, text in near-black `#111`, and a single signal red `#E4322B` that appears once per slide and only on the thing a reader must act on or remember (the revision token, the word "throw", the brew command); no gradients, no shadows, no light. Type is Inter Tight 800 for display, set flush-left ragged-right at 220 px with −5% tracking and 0.88 line height, bleeding off the top or left edge on alternate slides, with DM Mono 400 at 24 px for small captions pinned to grid baselines along the bottom margin like poster imprint lines. The gap is a single word, "Blind.", at 520 px, cropped by the right edge, with the caption "Agents write code. They can't see design."; the loop is three words, "Read. Change. Look.", stacked at 220 px with each followed by its verb in red mono at caption size; JavaScript is the real script set as a column of mono text on the right third, with "Measure after you write." in display on the left; the outlets slide is five columns of the grid each labeled with an outlet name, rotated 90° and set in display, like spines on a shelf; the reveal breaks the pattern by abandoning the grid entirely, the activity log running as a dense unjustified block of 14 px mono covering the whole page. Before reading, the reader sees a black shape too big to be text on cream, and a small red mark.

## Text and Pixels

The idea is the product's central claim made into layout: every slide is split vertically at 50%, the left half is what `tree` says, the right half is what `shot` shows, and the two halves describe the same thing, so the reader learns on every page that text can carry what a picture carries. The left ground is dark slate `#1B1E24` with mono text in `#D8DEE9`; the right ground is white, holding a rendered design; a single 2 px cyan `#3DDCFF` line runs horizontally across the seam at matching y-positions, tying a row in the tree to the node it names in the render, and that line is the only accent. Type is JetBrains Mono 400 at 22 px on the left, with the claim of each slide in Space Grotesk 600 at 64 px spanning the top 200 px across both halves on a band of `#0E1014`. The gap shows the right half full (a banking app screen from the `banking.pen` fixture) and the left half empty but for a cursor, "what the agent can see"; the loop puts the fixture's real `tree` output on the left and the render on the right with cyan leaders on three rows, then shows a `set` changing "Alex" to another name on both sides; JavaScript shows the script on the left and on the right the same card before and after, with a measured width annotation; the outlets slide keeps the left half as the single `.pen` tree and splits the right into a 2×2 of outputs (PNG, PDF page, React JSX, SwiftUI source) rendered as text and image tiles; the reveal breaks the pattern by making the right half this deck's own title slide and the left half its own `tree`. Before reading, the reader sees a hard dark–light split with bright horizontal threads crossing the middle.

## Diff

The idea is that the deck is a change set: each slide is presented as the diff from the previous one, so the story advances by additions and removals, which is how the reader already reviews an agent's work. The ground is GitHub-dark `#0D1117`, with removed lines on a `#F8514926` band and added lines on `#2EA04326`, gutter line numbers in 35% gray, and a file-header bar at the top of every slide reading `deck.pen` with the revision pair `a1b2… → c3d4…`; the only light is the tinted bands. Type is JetBrains Mono 400 at 28 px for the diff body and Inter 600 at 40 px for the header bar, nothing else. The gap is a removed line, `- // TODO: check it looks right`, and an added line that is only a question mark; the loop is a three-hunk diff, each hunk headed by the verb that produced it; JavaScript is a hunk that was rolled back, shown struck through with the note "script threw; nothing written"; the outlets slide is five added files in a file-tree sidebar, each with a +N count; the reveal breaks the pattern by collapsing the diff into its full history, the activity log shown as a commit list, every line attributed. Before reading, the reader sees red and green stripes on black: a code review.

## X-Ray

The idea is that the deck shows its own layout: every visible element on every slide wears a hairline bounding box and a small tag with its id and rect, exactly as `tree` reports them, so the reader sees the machine's view of each page on top of the human one and the thesis is never stated, only seen. The ground is a cool off-white `#EEF0F2`; content is ink `#14161A`; the annotation layer is a single magenta `#FF2E88` at 1 px stroke with 13 px tags on magenta fills, and absolute-positioned so it never disturbs layout; no shadows, no gradients. Type is Newsreader 500 at 88 px for claims (a serif to keep the human layer distinctly human), IBM Plex Mono 500 at 13 px for the tags and 24 px for any command. The gap slide has a headline and a single framed placeholder, and the annotation on the placeholder reads `frame ? 0,0 ?×?`: the agent cannot see it; the loop puts `tree` output in a column whose rows are themselves annotated, the text and its tags agreeing; JavaScript shows one headline whose width tag is measured and a magenta ruler asserting it fits, next to the script that made the check; the outlets slide places one small card five times, each tagged with the outlet it went to; the reveal breaks the pattern by hiding the content layer entirely, leaving only the magenta boxes, which still spell out the page, followed by the activity log that placed them. Before reading, the reader sees pale pages covered in fine pink rectangles, like an inspector left switched on.

## Revision Ledger

The idea is bookkeeping: the deck is a ledger of the writes that built it, every slide carries its own entry in a ruled footer (the revision token, the verb, the author given with `--as`, the time), and the reveal only has to turn the footers into the story. The ground is a warm ledger paper `#F3EEE3` with pale blue horizontal rules every 48 px and one red double vertical rule at x=160, drawn as paths; ink is `#1D2A3A`, the red `#B23A3A` used only for the rules and for reversals (an undo, a thrown script). Type is Fraunces 400 in the soft optical size at 96 px for each claim, set on the ruled baselines, with DM Mono 400 at 22 px for ledger entries in fixed columns. The gap is an entry with no amount: a row with a verb "write code", a checkmark, and a row "check design" with the amount column empty; the loop is three entries, `tree`, `set`, `shot`, each with its revision in the rev column and the `set` row showing the rev it was guarded by; JavaScript is an entry struck through in red with "rolled back; nothing written"; the outlets slide is one entry fanning into five sub-accounts; the reveal breaks the pattern by removing the claim and filling every ruled line of the page with this deck's real activity log, the footers of all previous slides now visible as rows in it. Before reading, the reader sees ruled paper, a red margin, and a column of small numbers at the bottom of every page.

## Title Card

The idea comes from Woodcase's first use case, agentic title design inside a video editor: every slide is a broadcast title card, a frame of video with title-safe guides, a timecode and a lower third, so the deck is a sequence of cards an agent set, and the reader meets the product in its native job. The ground is a deep broadcast black `#050505` with a subtle vignette made from a radial gradient, title-safe and action-safe guides drawn as 1 px `#FFFFFF1A` rectangles at 90% and 80%, and a timecode `00:00:0N:00` top-right in amber `#FFB000`; light is the vignette plus one horizontal linear-gradient bar behind each lower third. Type is Barlow Condensed 700 uppercase at 140 px, tracked +2%, for card titles, with Barlow 400 at 36 px for lower-third body and Space Mono 400 at 24 px for the timecode and commands. The gap card reads "NO PICTURE" centered in the safe area over a color-bars strip drawn as seven flat rectangles; the loop is three consecutive lower thirds on one card, each a verb and its output, like chapter supers; JavaScript is a card with a lower third that "fails safe", a red tally light (a filled circle) off and the text "script threw — no frame written"; the outlets slide is a multiview monitor wall, a 3×2 grid of small frames each labeled with an outlet; the reveal breaks the pattern by drawing the entire deck as a filmstrip, all the preceding cards in miniature along a timeline track, with the activity log as the edit decision list beneath. Before reading, the reader sees a dark screen with thin guides and a glowing lower third: television, which is strange enough to hold a minute.

## One Rectangle

The idea is the smallest possible demonstration: the whole deck is one rectangle, and each slide is that rectangle after one more `set`, so the story of the loop is told by a single node changing a single property at a time, and the text on each slide is only the command that produced it. The ground is pure white; the rectangle starts as a 1 px gray outline and ends, by the last slide, as a finished card in one saturated blue `#2F5BFF`; there is no light but what the rectangle acquires (an outer shadow appears on one slide as its own `set`). Type is none as design: every slide carries one line of JetBrains Mono 400 at 30 px bottom-left, the literal command, and nothing else, so type is not the point. The gap is the empty outline with the command `woodcase shot ?`; the loop is three slides, `tree` printing the rectangle's single row, `set` giving it a fill, `shot` naming the PNG; JavaScript is the one slide where the rectangle changes several properties at once, justified by a measure in the script, and the command is a single `woodcase js` line; the outlets slide breaks the pattern first, the rectangle shown five times, as pixels, as a vector PDF page's outline, as a JSX line, as a SwiftUI line and as a CGContext call; the reveal breaks it for good, the rectangle stepping aside to show this deck's full activity log, whose every line is one of the previous slides. Before reading, the reader sees a white page with a single shape on it, and on the next page, the same shape changed.

## One Canvas

The idea is that the deck is not nine pages but one large canvas seen through nine windows: a single wide composition of `tree` rows, nodes, arrows and renders, laid out as a map of the loop, and each 1920×1080 slide is a clipped frame onto a different region of it, so the reader moves across one argument rather than through separate ones, and the reveal zooms out. Each slide is a top-level frame with clip on, containing the same group at a different offset, which the renderer draws directly. The ground is a dark graphite `#17181B` with a 32 px dot grid drawn as small ellipses at 10% white, the canvas convention of every design tool; paths are 3 px `#9AA3AE`, and a single lime `#C6F432` marks the current window's subject. Type is Space Grotesk 500 at 72 px for region titles written into the canvas itself, and IBM Plex Mono 400 at 24 px for command output. The gap is the far-left region of the canvas, an agent's code on one side and a design frame on the other with a broken dashed path between them; the loop is a circular path in the canvas's center with `tree`, `set`, `shot` at three points and real output beside each; JavaScript is a region where a script's box sits inside the circle as a shortcut through it; the outlets region is the right side, one path splitting into five; the reveal breaks the pattern by removing the clip, the whole canvas scaled down to fit one page, the earlier windows outlined in lime and numbered, and the activity log running along the bottom as the route taken. Before reading, the reader sees a dotted dark field with fragments of shapes cut off at every edge, which promises there is more beyond the frame.
